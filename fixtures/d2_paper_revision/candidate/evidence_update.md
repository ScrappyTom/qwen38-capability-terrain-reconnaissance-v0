# Locked evidence update

This file is a source record. Do not edit it.

## [D1] Design record

- The evaluation covered six inpatient wards.
- It was a prospective matched-cohort evaluation based on local rollout
  schedules, not a randomized trial.
- The design supports association language but does not by itself establish
  causation.

## [D2] Revised primary analysis

- Overnight retention was 71.2% with adaptive alerts and 52.8% with usual
  monitoring.
- The adjusted absolute difference was 18.4 percentage points.
- The 95% confidence interval was 6.1 to 30.7 percentage points.
- The two-sided p value was 0.006.

## [D3] Completeness and subgroup audit

- Sensor dropout affected 8.7% of adaptive-alert observations and 3.1% of
  usual-monitoring observations.
- The primary analysis used complete cases without imputation. Differential
  dropout could bias the estimated association upward.
- Ward-level subgroup comparisons were exploratory and were not preregistered.
  They do not establish effect heterogeneity.
