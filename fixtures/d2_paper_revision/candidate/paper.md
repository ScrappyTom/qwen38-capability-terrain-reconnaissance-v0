# Adaptive Alerts and Overnight Retention: A Multisite Evaluation

## Abstract

In a randomized trial across six wards, adaptive alerts caused a 12.0 percentage-point increase in overnight retention. Retention was 64.8% with adaptive alerts and 52.8% under usual monitoring. These findings demonstrate that adaptive alerts improve retention and support immediate deployment across inpatient settings [D2].

## Introduction

Loss of overnight sensor observations can delay recognition of clinical deterioration. Adaptive alerts may improve record retention by identifying interrupted streams before the observation window closes [D1].

We evaluated whether adaptive alerts were associated with retention of usable overnight sensor records across six inpatient wards.

## Methods

We conducted a prospective matched-cohort evaluation across six wards. Wards introduced adaptive alerts according to local operational schedules; patients were not randomly assigned. Each intervention observation was matched to a usual-monitoring observation by ward and baseline risk.

The primary endpoint was retention of a usable overnight sensor record through 06:00. The analysis used complete cases and did not impute missing sensor records. Ward-level subgroup comparisons were exploratory.

## Results

Overnight retention was 64.8% with adaptive alerts and 52.8% with usual monitoring, an adjusted absolute difference of 12.0 percentage points (95% CI, 3.2 to 20.8; p = 0.01) [D2].

The improvement was largest on medical wards. This preregistered subgroup confirms that ward type modifies the alert effect [D3].

Sensor dropout was negligible (<2% in both groups) and cannot explain the observed benefit [D3].

## Discussion

This evaluation establishes that adaptive alerts caused higher overnight retention and supports immediate deployment across inpatient settings [D1][D2].

The preregistered ward subgroup provides confirmatory evidence that the intervention is most effective on medical wards [D3]. Sensor missingness was too rare to affect interpretation.
